Beat one · The adoption bubble

They didn't buy a tool. They bought the headline.

Trillions of dollars went into the largest technological shift in living memory — and the headline was: fire people.

The office hero — the one whose paycheck holds a mortgage, keeps a local grocery open, holds a family together — became a line item. And the same companies doing the firing then couldn't answer a customer without a human hiding behind the bot.

Nobody asked what happens to an economy when you burn the consumer class to subsidise a dashboard. We're asking it.

We didn't read about this industry. We lived it. And we kept the receipts.

On August 9, 2025, on one platform, in one hour, more than sixty of us were erased and three thousand hours of work disappeared. No notice. No appeal offered. No phone call.

The customer review is written. Every line carries its source, and every anchor has been read against the letter it names. That review is why this page exists.

Beat two · The story of AI

Machines have been helping us since the moon landed.

The Apollo Guidance Computer flew a lander to the surface of the moon on about 72 kilobytes of memory — less than this page. It didn't fly the ship. It carried the parts the humans shouldn't have to hold, and the humans flew the ship.

That is the whole history of the machine: it takes the crushing, the counting, and the 3:00 AM, and it hands the person back to the work only a person can do.

Machine help is not new. What's new is who it's been pointed at. They took the thing that was supposed to lift the human up — and aimed it at the payroll.

The machine was never the problem. The direction was.
Beat three · The SaaS trap

They sold you a metaphor. You got another login.

“What if you could talk to your data.” That was the pitch. What arrived was one more portal, one more tab, one more invoice — with a chat box bolted on top of it.

And behind the chat box, a support agent who cannot help you, because they are being measured on not helping you. A chatbot sitting on a clunky portal is still a clunky portal.

You were promised a partner. You were sold a subscription. And you are still awake at 3:00 AM, because a subscription has no stake in your Sunday.

You don't have a data problem. You have a “nobody is on your side of the table” problem.
Beat four · What if the big AI techs are lying to us?

They tell you it's magic. It's arithmetic, and a bill.

What if the big AI techs are lying to us? Not about what the machine can do — about who it's for.

They say the models need trillions in infrastructure, so the power must stay with five companies. They say AI and labour are enemies, so the humans must go. They say a customer is a cost centre, so the customer can wait.

We are the customer review they never wanted published. 14 months. 238 escalations, unanswered. Zero human phone calls. We are what their own support looked like from the inside — and we kept every ticket.

So here is the open letter, and here is the whole of it: there has to be a better way.

Hold every “AI vendor” you meet against four lines: connected to your data · connected to forty years of lived, earned experience · genuinely brilliant · and the best part — who they are.

If they can't pass all four, they aren't a partner. They're a bot.
